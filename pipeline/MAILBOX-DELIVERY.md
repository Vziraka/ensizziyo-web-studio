# Shared mailbox delivery

The `shared-mailbox-delivery-v1` profile adds explicit exact-message delivery to the canonical inbox/work/clock workspace. Old profiles remain unable to send. Content approval and send approval are distinct; AI proposals grant neither.

## Authority and persistence

Preparation retains the exact source sender and mailing address and binds account, grant, site, deploy, origin, recovery generation and finite hard limits in the immutable manifest. Schema-1 policies also keep their absolute proof expiry and lifetime intent cap. Delivery starts paused with no allowance. Signed operators review included usage, record a finite send allowance and resume explicitly. Each assigned operator reviews the complete message and PDF hashes before approving an individual send or selected scheduled outreach packages.

Dispatch rereads current source, ownership, approvals, pause, allowance and expiry before provider effects. Exact MIME is retained in immutable 1 MB chunks outside the 20 MB canonical state, then a permanent intent is reserved and claimed before the Gmail POST. Concurrent calls and lost acknowledgements cannot create another claim. Uncertain outcomes require original Sent readback; there is no resend button or automatic retry. Verified means exact sender-side evidence, not recipient inbox delivery. Replies retain their original thread; attachments retain original bytes, with a combined 20 MB PDF bound.

An existing intent response identifies an unclaimed reservation as an observation at that read; it does not prove another invocation cannot still claim it. The response marks it unresolved, says no retry is permitted and does not request a Sent readback until a claim is recorded. Refresh the saved intent if it changes; never resend. If a provider response is lost after a claim, the caller attempts to save the hold within its remaining invocation deadline; if the deadline or write outcome prevents that, the permanent claim remains uncertain and still requires original readback. Neither case refunds its charge.

Retained delivery history and its original readback control remain visible when an outreach batch is paused, requested-information content is replaced, or a reply conversation is stopped. Historical visibility does not enable a new send or settle the original intent.

Backups contain the exact MIME manifest/chunks. Recovery pauses delivery and invalidates old approvals; a changed deployment cannot dispatch old intents. Claimed historical intents can be reconciled read-only with the original account, including after allowance/policy expiry. Schema 1 requires the original grant; schema 2 can use an explicitly validated current grant for that same account after a reviewed upgrade. A different sender account is rejected. Missing or corrupted MIME fails closed before import writes.

A backup can predate a later reservation or even the first delivery ledger. Importing it into a different namespace does not fence a prior writer or prove that the imported history is complete. Before activating a successor, the release must establish and retain the exact current ledger, retire every prior writer with a verified conditional transition, account for in-flight effects, and prevent rollback or reactivation of old authority. The delivery state machine does not itself provide a global fence across namespaces; absent that evidence, keep successor delivery paused. A signed rebind or fresh allowance cannot establish it.

## Renewable manual allowances

Only the explicit operations capability may use delivery policy schema 2. It retains account/grant/site/deploy/origin/recovery/sender/kinds and `max_per_day`, and replaces `expires_at`/`max_intents` with `max_intents_per_allowance`, `max_allowance_minutes` and `max_retained_intents`. The stable policy is a hard ceiling, not execution authority. Old schemas/profiles keep their original limits. No recurring schedule is added by this change.

The existing signed allowance command keeps `{revision,allowance:{id,evidence_ref,observed_at,expires_at,included_only:true,max_intents}}`. A unique ID, observation at most fifteen minutes old, positive budget and finite expiry within the policy duration are required. All historical allowances remain. Renewal advances the send-approval epoch and requires fresh exact-send approval; it neither unpauses nor refunds any Reserved/Uncertain/Held/Verified intent. All sender-day charges and original reply/request/business deduplication span grants and policy history. Replacing or pausing authority fences an unused reservation on that canonical workspace key; it does not retire an independently writable namespace. An already claimed MIME can only be read back by its original identifiers, never replaced or resent.

Setup shows remaining allowance, daily and retained-history capacity, expiry and unresolved messages. History/storage full is an honest maintenance hold. Operators manually review included usage and the applicable reserve; the normal 30% reserve remains unchanged, with any explicit task exception recorded in the evidence reference. These controls do not read a live quota balance or authorize overage. Commercial outreach and requested information still require the verified mailing address; noncommercial replies remain a separate controlled journey.

A one-time new binding imports the exact G1 state unchanged, including nonempty legacy ledgers and MIME. Historical delivery stays read/reconcile-only before signed `/api/mailbox-delivery/rebind` names `{revision,previous_policy_sha256,authorized:true}` while paused. Rebind records old policy/account/grant/recovery identity, keeps paused, advances epoch and clears the active allowance pointer. It preserves every old intent, charge, approval, MIME/provider identity, status and receipt. A new finite allowance follows separately. Restore similarly revokes the active version-2 allowance pointer; historical charges and claims are never reset.

Typed replay and trusted-clock checks run again after awaits and at the actual SDK write admission. The existing Gmail adapter still checks current source/approval/allowance before its provider POST. The OAuth callback landing/script are static assets and open no canonical storage before authentication; owner/session/CSRF/PKCE checks remain on the callback POST.

## Hosting and operation

`EVZ_MAILBOX_DELIVERY_RUNTIME=shared-mailbox-delivery-v1` enables the signed dispatch/readback routes only on the exact protected published binding. The separate minute scheduler additionally requires `EVZ_MAILBOX_DELIVERY_CLOCK_RUNTIME=shared-mailbox-delivery-v1`; build uses `CONTEXT=production` and `EVZ_MAILBOX_DELIVERY_CLOCK_BUILD=shared-mailbox-delivery-v1`. Default/preview builds are disabled. Run `node scripts/build-desk-assets.mjs` from the repository root to emit the function entry.

Each scheduled invocation selects at most one due, separately approved outreach package inside its 60-second recipient-local slot and has a 25-second budget. It does not perform intake, make approvals or recover uncertain sends automatically. Mailbox intake remains the sole history cursor authority. New-business ceiling remains 100/day, with previously called businesses additional and one follow-up after seven days. A missed slot requires renewed review; no backlog burst. Multiple recipient timezones or overlapping additional slots can leave a shortfall, rather than sending extra messages later.

Commercial outreach requires a verified real mailing address in the exact policy and footer. The owner has no address; retain this hold without requesting it again. Finite allowances are reviewed limits, not live hosting/provider balance meters. Turning pause on prevents new effects and invalidates prior approvals; disabling the generated schedule via an inert publication stops recurring platform invocations. Preserve canonical records and original MIME during rollback.

## Bounded OAuth workspace validation

The default hosted manual dispatch/reconciliation and existing delivery-clock caller use `createGoogleOAuth().withMailboxOperation`. It checks the immutable workspace/configuration remotely at entry and exit and checks the same local binding throughout the awaited operation. `withMailboxRead` remains a compatible alias for intake. Neither scope caches credentials, grants, delivery state or approval decisions. The delivery service still reloads mutable authority around provider requests and before reservation/claim; conditional replay and write-boundary expiry checks remain unchanged. The hosted 25-second delivery budget starts before scope entry; an entry read that exhausts it prevents provider access. The exit check is awaited after the delivery operation and can complete or fail after that budget; it does not authorize another provider effect. A scope-exit failure after a send preserves the original intent and requires readback, never a replacement send.

Previously, one single-chunk Gmail request incurred 33 additional full workspace reads inside the OAuth checks, beyond the six mutable delivery guards. Existing small, injected-token fixtures did not expose that production cost. The regression now exercises the actual encrypted OAuth/default hosted path with finite synthetic storage latency. Its read counts and timing are fixture evidence, not a live latency guarantee. The 25-second delivery and provider deadlines are unchanged, and the scope adds no schedule, credential grant or sending authority.

## Delivery envelope witnesses

Delivery and original Sent reconciliation keep one validated envelope inside each invocation. Before every reuse they request strong-consistency metadata from the same store/key. An unchanged ETag witnesses the exact previously validated version; a changed version triggers a full read and validation. Missing metadata, storage errors and elapsed deadlines fail closed. Stores without metadata support retain full reads. Nothing is cached between invocations or across credential checks. Conditional transitions still independently read, validate and write with their original ETag and deadline guards. This does not authorize retrying a reserved or claimed intent.

The encrypted hosted regression at 750 ms per full-envelope read and 25 ms per metadata read changes from an uncertain result to exact Sent verification: 13 full reads and 46 metadata reads, 10.9 synthetic seconds. The retained-history fixture also exercises a legal 12 MB envelope. Synthetic time is not a production latency guarantee. Fresh production acceptance remains required; do not publish a replacement that invalidates the active encrypted Google binding without a coherent approved reconnect plan. No schema, grant, schedule, allowance or deadline changes are included. See the [Netlify metadata and strong consistency API](https://docs.netlify.com/build/data-and-storage/netlify-blobs/#getmetadata).
