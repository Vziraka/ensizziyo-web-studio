# Desktop call recording

The calling card has a **Record call** control. Claim the business, put the iPhone on speakerphone near the computer, confirm recording permission and press Record call. The browser asks for the computer microphone; no recording starts on page load. The active strip names the business and purpose, shows the timer and provides Stop recording even if another view is opened. Saving a call outcome waits until recording has stopped and its private save resolves.

This captures whatever the computer microphone hears. Both parties must be audible for both voices to be captured. It does not intercept an iPhone cellular connection, activate iPhone recording or read Apple Notes. Actual microphone permission, placement, room noise and both voices need an operator rehearsal before using it for a real call. The implemented browser route follows [getUserMedia](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/MediaDevices/getUserMedia) and [MediaRecorder](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/MediaRecorder); HTTPS or localhost is required.

After Stop, the clip is saved privately and appears with playback under that exact call. A failed or uncertain save retains the exact clip in the current tab, with retry and download controls; keep the tab open. A recorder error stops the microphone, retains available bytes for review and marks a manually saved clip as partial. Discard removes only this tab's copy; it does not delete a private reservation or saved history. Closing/reloading before a verified save can lose retained audio. The browser asks before leaving while audio remains active or unsaved.

If bytes were saved before their confirmation was interrupted, **Verify private audio save** checks the existing reservation, container and exact hash and finishes verification without uploading or recording again. It also works after reload. Missing or mismatched bytes remain held; no replacement is invented. Signing out stops active capture.

An uploaded recording may precede its call outcome. Its stable call ID survives a workspace refresh; History also exposes unlogged clips rather than silently attaching them to a different call. An undone call retains its audio and original IDs. A private upload does not invent a call attempt or change contact/sending rules.

## Spoken notes and transcripts

History has a separate **Record my spoken notes** control for an operator's private summary. Its audio is distinct from customer call audio and cannot be selected as customer-transcript evidence. Existing Dictate my post-call notes produces editable text; call recording must stop before dictation. Review and save that text before preparing an email revision.

Operational transcription remains manual. The finite [Gateway compatibility proof](AI-GATEWAY-PROOF.md) passed one short synthetic WAV through count/generation, separate from actual microphone or complete-call accuracy. No automatic Apple Notes import is connected. A permitted manual transcript may name selected stored call clips; the server verifies their exact call and purpose. Existing bounded transcript assessment, quote selection, human-reviewed wording and exact email release rules then apply. Private notes are not copied into customer email, uncertain claims require review, and new audio does not rewrite sent/reserved messages.

### Separate one-recording rehearsal

The saved connected call's History has **Read exact recording transcription policy** in controlled fictional workspaces only. Reading starts no inference. This is a separate, initially unapproved, **one-job lifetime rehearsal**, not reusable operational transcription. It cannot reopen, replace or refund the exhausted four-job Gateway proof. No hosted invocation or new allowance approval is established by implementation/tests.

The owner must separately review its exact policy/hash and complete saved fictional audio source, freshly verify at least 905 included credits (900 hosting reserve, the original four-credit proof reservation and this one credit) with auto recharge disabled, and explicitly confirm fictional speech only. Approval expires after fifteen minutes and does not start transcription. A separate Start button reserves one model credit durably before any provider request. One CountTokens request must stay within 4096 tokens before one generation capped at 1536 output tokens, no thinking/tools, no retries/redirects/provider fallback. It reuses the validated injected Gateway credentials and pinned SDK/model/catalog ceilings. Failures, lost responses, redeploys and Pause all retain the only reservation. Started provider work may finish or charge after pause.

The source must be exactly one entire, complete, permission-confirmed saved **call** clip (not private spoken notes), at most sixty seconds and 200 KB, in WAV/WebM/OGG. The private store verifies its bytes and SHA before model access; source metadata and the saved call are bound into approval. Partial, other-call, multi-clip, long, MP4, stopped/replied, undone or already-transcribed calls hold. These are rehearsal bounds, separate from the 4 MB/twenty-minute capture allowance. [Google's audio formats and token accounting](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/audio) support the candidate formats; actual Netlify pass-through has only been proved for the original short synthetic WAV. Do not relabel or convert MP4 implicitly.

Words and explicit uncertainty return as an **unreviewed Draft**. The operator checks playback, edits wording and confirms permission/uncertainty before adopting an exact receipt hash through the existing transcript event. The server rechecks call/audio identity and stores machine-source/draft/audio hashes and original uncertainty alongside the operator's wording. Adoption does not confirm identity, buying intent, quotes or facts. Existing transcript assessment and operator outcome/claim/email reviews follow; hosted assessment remains unconfigured. No transcription result or adoption changes an existing queued/native/sent email intent.

The protected routes are GET `/api/call-transcription`, POST `/api/call-transcription/approve` and POST `/api/call-transcription/run` (prefixed `/desk` on Netlify). No extra secrets or provider account are introduced. Local runtime credentials are absent by default; production workspaces and missing injected configuration fail closed. Ordinary hosted dashboards omit the raw rehearsal ledger; its dedicated authenticated view shows the exact source and receipt. Rollback retains its ledger and original audio/transcript provenance; never delete it to retry.

**Complete-call acceptance is still open.** It needs operator acoustic testing with both speakerphone voices; actual browser-format pass-through and noisy/uncertain speech checks; explicit treatment of every original clip/segment in order, including partial/missing segments; a verified decoding/chunking approach for twenty-minute recordings without dropped speech or silent scope expansion; durable per-segment and aggregate credit/token limits within the shared hosting allowance; and the real transcript-assessment/reviewed-email path. A short fixture cannot establish these. Real audio upload/inference requires a separately reviewed scope; neither this rehearsal nor stored recording consent authorizes it.

## Private storage and recovery

The server authenticates the operator and validates CSRF, project/call IDs, timestamps, permission, container type and size before reserving an audio ID. Only the trusted artifact route can record storage metadata. A reservation precedes binary storage; confirmed bytes, SHA-256, actor, purpose and times remain immutable. Exact retries converge on the same file and events. Uncertain writes retain their reservation and never overwrite another clip. Playback verifies the saved length and hash and requires the same private session. No clip bytes enter the public static site or Git.

Local audio lives under `call-audio/` in the private lifecycle directory. A fully written temporary file is linked to its immutable final ID without replacement, preventing partially written final files. Netlify uses new-only binary Blobs in the configured fictional namespace; deployed storage still needs its separate configured proof. Clips stop at twenty minutes or near 4 MB; continue with another clip when needed. Hard server ceilings are 4 MB per clip, 50 clips per Eastern day and 200 MB/2,000 clips retained across the workspace. These are conservative application guards, not a claim of hosting entitlement or capacity. No automatic deletion is introduced.

Local lifecycle backups now include verified stored audio in a sibling `<backup-file>.audio/` directory. Retain it with the JSON backup. Restore verifies every required clip before creating a new destination, refuses missing/altered/symlinked/oversized files, and restores with execution disabled. Reserved but unverified uploads remain unresolved; a backup does not turn them into a verified recording. Hosted backups, migration/retention operations and actual cloud storage remain separate acceptance work.
