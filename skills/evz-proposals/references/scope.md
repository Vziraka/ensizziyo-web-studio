# Scope that can be delivered and checked

Use the relevant service sections, not every section in every proposal. Discovery should establish the client's problem and current process before selecting tools. Treat portfolio concepts as demonstrations, not evidence of deployed integrations or client results.

## Shared scope

Define the outcome, concrete deliverables and quantities, client responsibilities, access/assets, exclusions, schedule dependencies, completion checks, ownership/handover and ongoing responsibility. Set one feedback decision-maker and allocate the two project revision rounds. Timelines start from the agreed inputs/access/payment milestone; discuss how delays change dates. The master's two-business-day feedback window is an example, not an imposed universal term.

An improvement in usability or a working workflow can be a deliverable. Guaranteed revenue, lead volume, search ranking or time savings cannot be inferred from the proposal.

## Websites and landing pages

List actual pages and functions. A five-page site does not include unlimited service pages, copywriting, a CMS, commerce or a portal. Define copy editing versus writing, photographs/asset quantities, responsive behavior, forms, search metadata and hosting/launch. Confirm required policy content and who supplies it.

Agree the selected direction and preserve client branding/assets as appropriate. Budget limits features and effort, not the care put into typography, layout and content. Do not automatically add the separate five-direction company-concept workflow to a fixed website package.

Completion checks should cover relevant desktop/mobile layouts, keyboard navigation, links, forms and real authorized delivery paths. Separate basic on-page setup from ongoing SEO. Preserve an existing site's content/URLs or define migration/redirect work explicitly when replacing it.

## Systems, CRM and integrations

Name the platforms, users/locations, pipeline stages, fields, permissions and connections. Distinguish configuring existing software from building custom software. Define record volumes/cleanup if importing data, matching/deduplication rules, ownership, error alerts, retry/recovery and training.

Replace vague "CRM integration" language with the actual trigger, fields, destination, expected action and acceptance checks. Check platform/plan compatibility before final approval. Do not copy Pipedrive, Make or any other tool from the fictional sample without a project reason.

## Automation and AI

For each workflow specify trigger, inputs, steps/branches, destinations, stop conditions, duplicate prevention, failure behavior and human responsibility. Bound custom APIs, authenticated access, historic migration and ongoing monitoring.

For an AI assistant specify channel (website chat versus voice), approved knowledge, allowed actions, fallback/human handoff, test scenarios, prohibited commitments and ongoing knowledge ownership. Scope calendar/CRM integrations separately enough to understand their cost. Identify relevant privacy, recording/consent and data-handling requirements for the actual use rather than promising compliance from a platform badge. No invented accuracy guarantees or autonomous decision authority.

For voice, discuss business hours, routing, transfer/fallback, expected call volume/minutes, phone numbers, languages and whether booking is included. State the setup fee separately from vendor usage and optional EVZ care.

## Marketing

Identify audience, campaign objective, channels, deliverable counts/formats, supplied assets, copy, review rounds and final files. Distinguish original creative from resizing/adaptation. The content starter is six static assets/captions, not video production, social inbox management or a full campaign operation.

For ongoing management, define cadence, channels, publishing responsibility, reports, replies/moderation and term. For SEO, specify audit/fixes/content/local listing work and reporting; no guaranteed positions. For ads, separate setup, creative, tracking, optimization and reporting from ad spend. Confirm account ownership, budget and permissions; a proposal does not authorize campaign activation.

## Completion and support

Use observable checks linked to deliverables. Obtain client written approval before launch; no automatic acceptance through silence. Distinguish refinements within scope, out-of-scope requests and defects against the agreed scope.

The handover should include agreed source/files, client account access, relevant documentation and one training session. Explain third-party licenses and responsibility after the 30-day defect period. Ongoing care needs its own bounded scope and price.

Do not automatically turn the proposal into a comprehensive legal services contract. Preserve approved commercial terms, identify material missing agreement terms for the owner's review, and avoid adding liabilities, penalties or intellectual-property promises without discussion.
