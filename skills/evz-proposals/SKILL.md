# EVZ Proposals

Produce a detailed, readable proposal for a small business: one recommended solution with relevant optional add-ons. Use the owner's approved EVZ master design and commercial defaults. This skill is for proposals after discovery, not speculative prospect outreach or website mockup exploration.

## Discuss before creating

**Do not create a client-facing proposal, including a draft PDF, document, deck, email proposal or client-addressed prose, until the owner explicitly approves that project's final scope and price.** Approval of this skill, its master template, package starting prices, or another client's quote is not project approval. A request such as "make a proposal for Company X" starts preparation and discussion; it does not supply the missing approval.

Read discovery notes and relevant supplied resources. Resolve facts independently where possible; do not repeat settled interview questions. Read [pricing and commercial rules](references/pricing.md) and the relevant portions of [scope guidance](references/scope.md).

Prepare a concise **internal owner review** in chat or a private working note:
- Client, problem, recommended outcome and bounded deliverables.
- Exclusions, client inputs, tool/account assumptions, acceptance checks and timeline.
- Starting package, adjustments and reasons, proposed fixed total in USD, exact payment amounts, optional add-ons and third-party/recurring costs.
- Revision allocation, defect support, handover and any material unresolved questions.

Ask the owner to approve the exact scope and price, or discuss changes. This approval is required by the owner's explicit instruction. Continue independent research while waiting, but do not generate the client proposal. An ambiguous reply or elapsed time is not approval. Record the approved scope/version, total, options, commercial terms and the owner's approval message in the private project notes. Existing explicit approval in the current project context counts; do not ask again unnecessarily.

If price, deliverables, exclusions, responsibilities or material terms change after approval, discuss the changes and obtain approval before revising the client proposal. Ordinary wording, formatting and corrections that restore the approved scope do not need another approval.

## Author the approved proposal

Read [document design and output](references/document.md). Use the available PDF skill for authoring and rendering; bundled assets make the design portable. The approved fictional master is a visual reference, not client evidence. Replace all sample identities, dates, amounts, tools and assumptions with approved project content.

Keep detail proportional to the project. Describe outcomes, exact deliverables, boundaries, inputs, milestones, investment, revisions, completion checks, handover and support. Explain technical detail only when it defines what the client receives or must do. Do not invent testimonials, capabilities, expected ROI, guaranteed leads, client agreement or signatures.

Keep client notes, approval records, source data and outputs in an authorized private workspace outside the studio Git repository. Save editable source with the PDF. Review every rendered page, reconcile all amounts and check against the approved scope before delivery. Report actual checks and any remaining limitation. Creating a proposal does not authorize sending it, publishing it, enrolling the client in software or creating a contract/invoice.
