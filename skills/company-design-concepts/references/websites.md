# Website directions

Use for the initial website PDF and later selected-direction design refinement.

## First comparison

Create **five distinct desktop directions** for the same company and business goal. Each shows a designed opening and **2-3 key sections** that make the offer and customer action understandable. Choose the sections from the actual business: a useful product selection, service coverage, project examples, ordering steps, menu, visit information, or other relevant content.

Use the shared [art-direction process](art-direction.md): research, image-generated exploration, internal critique, faithful composition with original assets, then comparison against the retained targets. Five is the final review count, not a limit on internal exploration or a requirement to deliver the first five outputs. Give each direction a short name and a visible premise that shapes the page. Explore familiar and adventurous designs at equal quality; recommend the best fit after seeing the work.

Use the company's original photos and unchanged logo inside the final layouts. Explore colors and fonts unless explicitly constrained; permitted supporting artwork can extend the identity. Strong photography should shape the composition; select and crop images deliberately instead of repeating a decorative hero across every section.

Design the opening and its 2-3 sections as a continuous visual argument. Carry the premise into the way work, services, details or the customer journey are presented. Vary scale and density with purpose; avoid automatically assigning every direction a split hero, equal service-card row, image/text band and contact strip. Conventional structures can serve a direction when justified, but they cannot be the shared recipe for the entire set.

Show real website design, not only a palette, wireframe, mood board, or written description. Keep important words legible and buttons meaningful. Use realistic desktop proportions; mark or explain the first-screen boundary when presenting a longer page. Keep the following sections visible without squeezing a whole scroll journey into one implausible viewport.

The first review is **desktop only**. Do not generate phone variants automatically. The user's chosen direction is the starting point for later phone layouts and more complete page designs.

## Explain each direction

Include the concept name, what visibly makes it different, why it fits this business, a short reference-to-design connection, the actual type choices, and a useful tradeoff. Brief proposed arrival/scroll/control behavior can explain an interactive idea; identify it as proposed motion. Avoid claiming a still image demonstrates animation or a working booking/order flow.

For a prospect, lead with the visual and a short explanation of the customer benefit. For a paying client, explain the hierarchy, chosen content, image treatment, and important design decisions in more detail. The PDF reference defines shared packaging.

Compare the five fairly, recommend the strongest direction for the stated goal, and invite selection or specific elements to combine. Keep concept names stable through feedback.

## After selection

Preserve the selected composition, original company images, and accepted wording. Refine the requested parts rather than generating an unrelated replacement. Develop a deliberate phone layout after selection, including reflow, readable type, image crops, and the main customer action; a desktop crop is not a phone design.

Expand to complete homepage or supporting-page studies when requested. Website implementation remains a follow-on task with its own scope and verification.
