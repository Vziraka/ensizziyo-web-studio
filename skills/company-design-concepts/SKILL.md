# Company Design Concepts

Create business-specific, visual PDF concept reviews using the company's actual imagery and information. These defaults come from the owner's October 20, 2026 interview; honor later explicit changes without repeating that interview.

## Choose the requested work

- **Website:** five distinct desktop directions, each showing the opening plus 2-3 key sections. Read [website directions](references/websites.md). Develop phone layouts after a direction is selected.
- **Campaign:** three promotional ideas for the client company's products, services, offers, or events. Each has a sample social post, ad, and matching landing-page concept. Read [campaign directions](references/campaigns.md). Deliver ideas and sample designs in a PDF, not a finished campaign asset package.
- Run both modes when requested. A website request alone does not add a campaign. If concept intent is already established and the user supplies only a company resource, default to website mode and state that assumption.
- Use a concise visual pitch for prospects and fuller reasoning/design notes for paying clients. Infer the audience from context; otherwise state a concise-prospect-format assumption and proceed.

The initial output is static design. Code used to compose or render mockups does not change the deliverable into an implemented website. Hosting is unnecessary for this PDF workflow. Production builds, publishing, campaign execution, and external messages are separate requests.

## Research before interviewing

Inspect the supplied website, public social profile, listing, document, photos, or other resource directly. Follow relevant official links to establish the correct company and inspect its products/services, customer journey, photography, brand cues, recent activity, and evergreen content. Visually inspect suitable images; text extraction and filenames alone cannot establish their content.

Form a compact working brief: business and audience, offer, useful proof, primary customer action, brand constraints, source images, and consequential gaps. Link sources and record dates for information that may change. Verify current offers and factual claims against the company's own material; distinguish an observed fact from an inference or a proposed idea.

Research independently, then ask only unanswered questions that would materially change the designs. Examples include ambiguous company identity, an unknown campaign offer, incompatible brand constraints, or unavailable essential photos. Ask plainly and in small rounds, explain unfamiliar terms, and recommend a choice when useful. Do not ask the user to supply facts that accessible sources already establish or re-interview settled preferences.

If a resource is blocked or requires unavailable access, try appropriate public first-party alternatives. Explain remaining gaps and use explicit assumptions for nonessential details. Request accessible material only when needed for a faithful result; never claim to have inspected an inaccessible feed or document.

## Preserve the business and its assets

- Keep the company's actual logo and recognizable brand cues. Explore layout, typography, hierarchy, composition, and color treatments within that identity. A broader rebrand needs a request.
- Use the company's original image files directly in final mockups where relevant and available. Preserve the source files and the depicted products, people, packaging, and work; cropping and proportional scaling are suitable layout operations.
- An image attached to an AI generation is a reference, not proof that the final depiction is unchanged. Choose an authoring route that can place originals directly, such as PDF or static HTML/CSS composition. Image generation can help ideation, but redrawn approximations do not satisfy the original-image requirement.
- Design around weak or small images with suitable crops, scale, spacing, and other real assets. If a supporting image is still needed, use an appropriate licensed stock placeholder for general atmosphere and identify it in the review notes. Keep the company's products, people, premises, and completed work accurately attributed. Flag important missing assets. Generated supporting imagery is a separately requested exception.
- Write clearer headlines, descriptions, and buttons using verified business information. Preserve explicit approved wording. Do not invent prices, discounts, capabilities, certifications, customer quotations, metrics, dates, or endorsements to complete a composition; omit them, label a proposal, or ask when consequential.

## Design, compare, and deliver

Use the relevant mode reference to create the actual visual studies. Explore a range from polished and familiar to bold and distinctive, suited to the audience and business. Keep every option credible and worth choosing; avoid a fixed set of five styles reused for every company. Inspect relevant outside design references when they help, applying principles rather than copying another business's identity or composition.

Read [PDF review and handoff](references/pdf-review.md) before composing the review. Use the available PDF skill and its render-and-inspect workflow. If using ImageGen or Creative Production, load the relevant available skill and follow its tool requirements. Reuse available capabilities; a catalog link does not imply installed tools, account access, or spending authorization.

Keep client research, image files, source notes, and generated reviews in the authorized client/task workspace outside the studio Git repository. The repository skill contains reusable instructions only. Retain enough source and selection context to refine an approved direction without silently redesigning it.

Deliver the PDF with a short recommendation, meaningful tradeoffs, and the next selection to make. Report what was actually produced and visually checked. Do not claim responsive behavior, implemented motion, production-ready ads, or publication rights from static studies.
